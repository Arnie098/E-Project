                                              INTRODUCTION

                                        Background of the Study

         Cultural preservation in the Philippines is strongly supported by national policies
and institutions tasked with safeguarding the country's diverse heritage. The National
Cultural Heritage Act (RA 10066) provides a legal framework for the protection and
conservation of cultural properties, while agencies such as the National Commission for
Culture and the Arts and the National Museum of the Philippines implement programs
that promote indigenous traditions, languages, and historical sites. These efforts include
documentation, conservation of artifacts, and educational campaigns aimed at raising
public awareness. However, despite these initiatives, challenges such as rapid
urbanization, globalization, and limited resources continue to threaten cultural heritage.
In recent years, the integration of digital technologies has emerged as a promising
approach to preservation. Digital platforms, online repositories, and information systems
now play a crucial role in organizing, storing, and disseminating cultural knowledge,
enabling wider access and long-term sustainability. Scholarly studies on digital
preservation emphasize that technology, when combined with community participation,
enhances the protection and transmission of indigenous knowledge systems.

         The Philippines is home to more than 175 ethnolinguistic groups, reflecting its rich
cultural diversity and deep historical roots. Among these groups are the Bagobo people
of southern Mindanao, composed of subgroups such as the Tagabawa, Clata (Guiangan),
and Ubo. These communities share a common identity yet maintain distinct dialects,
traditions, and artistic expressions. Historically, the Bagobo have inhabited areas
surrounding the Davao Gulf and the slopes of Mount Apo, where their cultural practices
have been preserved through oral traditions and community life. However, globalization
and modernization have significantly influenced cultural dynamics, leading to shifts in
language use and lifestyle, particularly among the younger generation. This study, which
proposes the development of a comprehensive website framework for the preservation
and revitalization of the Bagobo dialect and cultural heritage, responds to the growing
need for innovative solutions. Existing research supports the use of digital platforms as
effective tools for cultural education, engagement, and preservation, highlighting their
ability to connect traditional knowledge with modern audiences while promoting
inclusivity and accessibility.

         Despite the presence of legal frameworks and cultural programs, the Bagobo
language and cultural heritage are increasingly at risk of decline. Factors such as
migration, exposure to dominant cultures, and the preference for widely used languages
have contributed to the gradual loss of indigenous linguistic practices. Younger
generations, in particular, are becoming less familiar with their native dialect, leading to
a weakening of cultural identity and continuity. Furthermore, there is a lack of centralized,
accessible, and interactive platforms dedicated to preserving and teaching the Bagobo
dialect and traditions. Existing resources are often scattered, limited in scope, or not
easily accessible to the community. This gap highlights a critical problem: without
effective and sustainable preservation strategies, the Bagobo language and its associated
cultural knowledge may continue to diminish, potentially leading to cultural erosion over
time.

         In response to these challenges, this study proposes the development of a digital
website platform that serves as both a repository and an interactive learning environment
for the Bagobo dialect and cultural heritage. The proposed system aims to integrate
features such as multimedia content, language tutorials, and community participation
tools to encourage active engagement and knowledge sharing. This initiative aligns with
the objectives of the Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act (IPRA) 8371, which emphasizes the
protection and promotion of indigenous cultural rights and identity. By leveraging
technology, the platform seeks to ensure the sustainable preservation of cultural
resources while fostering intergenerational transmission of knowledge. Ultimately, the
study envisions creating an inclusive, accessible, and community-centered solution that
not only preserves the Bagobo heritage but also strengthens cultural pride and identity
in the modern digital era.

                                           Scope and Limitation

         This study focuses on the development of a web-based cultural preservation
system for the Bagobo Tagabawa community, aimed at documenting and promoting their
language, traditions, and cultural heritage through a centralized digital platform. The
system includes features such as a digital repository, interactive learning modules, role-
based access control, and an AI-assisted learning component. It is designed to be user-
friendly and accessible, allowing both community members and learners to engage with
cultural content. While primarily focused on the Bagobo Tagabawa culture, the system
also has potential for future expansion to other indigenous communities.

         However, the study is limited by several factors, including restricted internet access
and device availability in rural areas, which may affect user participation. The AI
functionality is limited to basic assistance features and does not include advanced artificial
intelligence capabilities. Additionally, the system's content is dependent on available data
collected during development, and continuous updates require further support.
Evaluation is also limited to selected software quality attributes and respondents, while
long-term maintenance and scalability are beyond the scope of the study.

                                        Significance of the Study

         This study is significant as it contributes to the preservation and revitalization of
the Bagobo Tagabawa dialect and cultural heritage through a web-based digital platform.
It provides a centralized system for documenting, storing, and accessing cultural
resources, helping prevent the loss of indigenous knowledge caused by modernization
and globalization. It is very significant to this following:

         Bagobo Community. The system serves as a digital archive and learning tool
that helps preserve their language, traditions, oral narratives, and cultural identity. It also
promotes intergenerational knowledge transfer by allowing elders to share cultural
knowledge and enabling younger generations to learn and engage with their heritage in
an interactive and meaningful way.

         Students. The system provides a centralized and organized source of cultural and
linguistic data that can be used for academic studies, cultural research, and language
learning. It simplifies access to information that is often scattered or difficult to obtain,
thereby supporting educational and research activities related to indigenous culture.

         Educational Institutions. The study offers a supplementary learning resource
that can be integrated into cultural education programs, promoting awareness and
appreciation of indigenous heritage among learners. It also supports innovative teaching
approaches through interactive and technology-based learning tools.

         LGU's and Cultural Organizations. The system provides a potential tool for
cultural preservation initiatives and community development programs. It supports the
objectives of safeguarding indigenous identity and can assist in policy implementation
related to cultural conservation.

         Future Researchers and Developers. This study serves as a reference for
creating similar systems that combine cultural preservation with web-based technologies
and AI integration. It provides a foundation for further improvements and expansion to
other indigenous communities and cultural contexts.

                                            Definition of Terms
         This study is composed of terminologies that are commonly used particularly in
the context of this study.
         Artificial Intelligence (AI) ­ A system feature that uses intelligent algorithms
(e.g., chatbot or recommendation system) to provide adaptive learning support and user
assistance.
         Admin ­ A user role responsible for managing and uploading content, monitoring
system activity, and maintaining the platform.
         Adaptive Learning ­ A learning approach where the system adjusts content and
difficulty based on the user's performance and progress.

         Bagobo - One of the indigenous ethnolinguistic groups in Mindanao, Philippines,
known for their rich cultural traditions, oral literature, rituals, and distinct dialect.

         Bagobo Tagabawa ­ one of the distinct dialects that the Bagobo indigenous
group has.

         Chatbot Tutor ­ An AI-based tool designed to interact with users, answer
questions, provide explanations, and assist in learning the Bagobo dialect.

         Cultural Heritage ­ The traditions, customs, language, beliefs, and material
expressions inherited from previous generations that define the identity of a community.

         Digital Preservation ­ The use of digital technologies to collect, store, manage,
and safeguard cultural and historical resources for long-term accessibility and protection.

         Digital Repository ­ A structured online storage system used for organizing and
managing digital files such as documents, images, audio recordings, and videos for easy
access and retrieval.

         Web-Based System ­ A software application that runs on a web browser and
can be accessed through the internet or local network without requiring installation on a
device.
         Role-Based Access Control ­ A security framework that restricts system access
based on user roles and permissions to ensure proper data management and system
security.

         Super Admin ­ The highest-level user with full administrative control over the
system, including user management, system configuration, and content approval.

         User ­ An end-user who accesses the system primarily for learning, viewing
cultural content, and interacting with the learning modules.

         Interactive Learning Module ­ A feature of the system that provides structured
educational content such as lessons, quizzes, and activities to enhance user engagement
and learning experience.

         Repository Management ­ The process of organizing, uploading, updating, and
maintaining digital content within the system's database.

         User Interface (UI) ­ The visual layout and design of the system that allows
users to interact with its features and functions.

         User Experience (UX) ­ The overall experience of users when interacting with
the system, including ease of use, accessibility, and satisfaction.

         System ­ A structured set of interconnected components (hardware, software,
and processes) that work together to perform specific functions, such as managing data,
processing information, and delivering services to users within a defined environment.

Digital Cultural Preservation System
         Digital preservation has become an essential strategy in safeguarding indigenous

knowledge and cultural heritage in the modern era. With the increasing impact of
globalization, many indigenous languages and traditions are at risk of being lost, making
digital documentation a necessary intervention.

         According to UNESCO (2020), digital technologies play a significant role in
preserving intangible cultural heritage by enabling systematic documentation, storage,
and wider dissemination of cultural materials.

         Similarly, Kurniawan and Hartati (2021) emphasized that digital archiving systems
ensure the long-term sustainability of cultural resources such as oral traditions, language
recordings, and historical documents.

         Furthermore, Smith (2022) noted that digital preservation enhances the visibility
and accessibility of indigenous cultures, although it requires active community
participation to maintain authenticity and cultural sensitivity. These findings highlight the
importance of developing a digital platform that not only preserves cultural heritage but
also ensures its accessibility for future generations.

Web-Based Systems for Cultural and Educational Platforms
         Web-based systems have become widely utilized in both educational and cultural

preservation initiatives due to their accessibility, scalability, and flexibility. These systems
allow users to access information anytime and anywhere, making them highly effective
tools for knowledge dissemination.

         Kumar and Singh (2020) stated that web-based platforms provide efficient and
scalable solutions for delivering content, particularly in educational environments.

         In addition, Al-Fraihat et al. (2020) found that the success of web-based learning
systems is largely influenced by system quality, information quality, and service quality,
which directly impact user satisfaction and engagement. Their study further emphasized
that interactive features and well-designed interfaces enhance user experience and
learning outcomes. These findings support the use of a web-based system in cultural
preservation, as it enables wider access and promotes user engagement in learning
indigenous culture.

Digital Repositories and Information Management Systems
         Digital repositories play a crucial role in managing and preserving large volumes

of data in an organized and accessible manner. These systems provide structured storage,
efficient retrieval, and long-term preservation of digital content.

         According to Rodrigues et al. (2020), digital repositories utilize features such as
metadata tagging, indexing, and search functionalities to improve data accessibility and
management.
         Similarly, Khan and Bhatti (2021) highlighted that centralized repository systems
enhance knowledge sharing and ensure data consistency, which are essential for
sustainable information management. Their study also emphasized that repositories are
particularly valuable in preserving cultural and academic resources. These findings justify
the integration of a centralized digital repository in the proposed system to ensure the
efficient storage and retrieval of Bagobo cultural materials.

AI in Education and Cultural Learning
         Artificial Intelligence (AI) has significantly transformed educational systems by

introducing adaptive and interactive learning experiences. AI-powered tools, such as
chatbots and recommendation systems, have been widely used to enhance user
engagement and personalize learning.

         Zawacki-Richter et al. (2020) explained that AI enables adaptive learning by
analyzing user behavior and adjusting content according to individual needs and
performance.

         Furthermore, Winkler and Söllner (2020) found that chatbot-based learning
systems improve interaction by providing instant responses and continuous support to
users.

         In addition, Okonkwo and Ade-Ibijola (2021) concluded that AI chatbots are
particularly effective in language learning, as they simulate conversational interactions
and provide real-time feedback. These studies support the integration of an AI-driven
component in the system to enhance the learning experience and promote engagement
in cultural education.

User Experience and UX Design in Web Applications
         User experience (UX) design is critical in determining the success and usability of

web-based systems. According to Garrett (2020), a well-designed user interface enhances
usability by making systems intuitive, efficient, and easy to navigate.

         In addition, a study by Hassenzahl and Tractinsky (2021) emphasized that positive
user experience increases user satisfaction and engagement, which are essential for
system adoption. These findings suggest that the proposed system must prioritize user-
friendly design to ensure accessibility for both technical and non-technical users.

Role Based Access Control in Web System
         Security and proper access management are essential components of web-based

systems, especially those handling sensitive or valuable data. Role-Based Access Control
(RBAC) is a widely used security model that restricts system access based on user roles
and permissions.

         According to Sandhu et al. (2020), RBAC ensures that users can only access
information relevant to their roles, thereby improving system security and efficiency.
         Similarly, Hu et al. (2021) emphasized that RBAC reduces the risk of unauthorized
access and simplifies system administration by clearly defining user responsibilities. This
model is particularly effective in systems with multiple user roles, such as administrators
and general users. These findings support the implementation of RBAC in the proposed
system to ensure secure and organized management of user access.

Community Participation & Language Preservation
         Community involvement is a key factor in the success of cultural preservation

initiatives. According to Giaccardi and Palen (2020), participatory systems that involve
local communities in content creation and management ensure authenticity and
sustainability. Indigenous knowledge is best preserved when elders and cultural
custodians actively contribute to the documentation process.

         Similarly, a study by Brown and Nicholas (2021) emphasized that community-
driven digital platforms empower indigenous groups by giving them control over how
their culture is represented. These findings highlight the importance of incorporating user
participation features in the system to encourage collaboration and cultural ownership.

The reviewed literature shows a strong connection between digital technology and
cultural preservation. Digital repositories and web-based systems provide efficient
platforms for storing and sharing cultural data, while AI technologies enhance user
engagement and learning. This study builds upon these concepts to address the
preservation and revitalization of the Bagobo Tagabawa dialect and cultural heritage.

Mukurtu CMS
         Christen et al. (2017) developed Mukurtu CMS as an open-source content
management system designed specifically for Indigenous communities to manage and
protect digital cultural heritage. The system integrates cultural protocols that regulate
access, sharing, and use of sensitive cultural materials based on community-defined rules.
This study highlights that digital heritage systems can be effectively designed using
Indigenous governance principles, ensuring that cultural ownership and control remain
with the community while still enabling digitization and preservation of cultural resources.

Marayum
         The University of the Philippines Diliman and DOST-ASTI developed Marayum

Dictionary Platform as a collaborative online dictionary platform for Philippine Indigenous
languages. The system enables crowdsourced contributions where native speakers can
encode, validate, and refine language entries. This study demonstrates that community-
driven digital platforms can play a significant role in preserving endangered languages by
promoting participatory documentation and continuous language development.

Higaonon Mobile Dictionary
         Researchers under MDPI (2025) developed Higaonon Mobile Dictionary as a mobile

application aimed at preserving the Higaonon language in Mindanao. The application
provides digitized vocabulary, pronunciation guides, and audio-based learning materials
to support language retention and learning. This study shows that mobile-assisted
language learning systems can improve accessibility and contribute to the revitalization
of endangered Indigenous languages, particularly among younger generations.

FirstVoices
         The First Peoples' Cultural Council developed FirstVoices as a web-based platform

for the documentation, preservation, and teaching of Indigenous languages. The system
provides integrated tools such as dictionaries, keyboards, audio recordings, and
storytelling archives to support both linguistic preservation and education. This study
emphasizes that comprehensive digital language platforms are effective in combining
archival preservation with interactive learning functionalities.

Irula Language MALL
         The Cureus Journal (2025) presented Irula Language Mobile Assisted Language

Learning App as a gamified mobile-assisted language learning application designed for
the Irula tribal language. The system incorporates quizzes, rewards, and interactive
exercises to enhance user engagement and learning retention. This study demonstrates
that gamification techniques in mobile learning environments can significantly improve
language acquisition, especially in the context of Indigenous and endangered languages.

Respectful Design Framework
         Reitsma et al. (2013) proposed the Respectful Design Framework as a participatory
approach to system development that prioritizes Indigenous ownership and involvement.
The framework emphasizes co-design processes where communities actively participate
in decision-making throughout system development. This study highlights the importance
of ethical and culturally sensitive design in technology development for Indigenous
knowledge systems.

WordWeaver
         The National Research Council of Canada developed WordWeaver as an open-

source linguistic tool that assists in the analysis and generation of complex verb structures
in Indigenous languages. The system supports linguists and researchers in developing
accurate language resources and documentation tools. This study shows that
computational linguistic systems can enhance the structural analysis and preservation of
endangered languages.

Digital Preservation of IK Framework
         Biyela et al. (2016) developed the Digital Preservation of Indigenous Knowledge

Framework as a structured model for digitizing Indigenous knowledge systems. The
framework emphasizes ethical documentation, cultural context preservation, and
community participation in all stages of digital archiving. This study highlights that
successful preservation of Indigenous knowledge requires balancing technological
innovation with cultural integrity and participatory governance.

Dive Into Heritage
         UNESCO (2024) developed Dive into Heritage as a digital platform that utilizes 3D

modeling and virtual reality to document and present cultural heritage sites and intangible
cultural practices. The system enables immersive exploration of heritage content for
educational and preservation purposes. This study demonstrates that immersive
technologies such as VR can enhance cultural education and improve engagement with
heritage preservation initiatives.

Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL)
         The Government of India established Traditional Knowledge Digital Library as a

digital repository for documenting and protecting traditional medicinal knowledge
systems such as Ayurveda, Unani, and Siddha. The system serves as a defensive
mechanism against biopiracy by providing documented evidence of prior traditional
knowledge. This study shows that digital knowledge repositories can effectively safeguard
Indigenous and traditional knowledge from unauthorized patent claims.
The reviewed systems show a trend toward community-based, interactive, and ethical
approaches in preserving language and culture. However, there is still a gap in fully
integrating Indigenous dialects with AI-powered and culturally adaptive learning systems,
which this study aims to address.

                                         Conceptual Framework
         The conceptual framework of the study illustrates the overall process involved in
the development of the Web-Based Bagobo Tagabawa Cultural Preservation System.
The framework begins with identifying the cultural and language preservation needs of
the Bagobo Tagabawa community, particularly the risk of losing their dialect and
cultural heritage due to modernization. To address these concerns, the system gathers
cultural data through web platform inputs, audio and video recordings, document
uploads, and community contributions.
         The collected information is organized and stored in a centralized digital
repository that serves as a secure database for cultural and language resources. To
ensure authenticity and accuracy, the data undergo validation and monitoring through
the participation of elders and cultural custodians. After validation, the information is
processed by the AI Learning and Analytics Engine, which provides features such as AI
chatbot support, language learning assistance, recommendation systems, and pattern
analysis to improve user interaction and learning experiences.
         The processed data are then utilized through the Decision Support module and
the Web-Based Dashboard, which provide reports, monitoring tools, and cultural
insights for users and administrators. The framework also emphasizes cultural
implementation and community engagement through learning activities, cultural
promotion, and language revitalization initiatives. Lastly, a feedback and continuous
improvement loop ensures that user input and community updates are continuously
integrated into the system to support the long-term preservation and sustainability of
Bagobo Tagabawa culture and language.

                                               METHODOLOGY
         This chapter presents the methodology used in developing the proposed Web-
Based Bagobo Tagabawa Cultural Preservation System. It discusses the research design,
data gathering procedures, development process, and technologies used to ensure the
effective preservation and revitalization of the Bagobo Tagabawa dialect and cultural
heritage.

                                              Capstone Locale
         The study will be conducted in the Municipality of Bansalan, Davao del Sur,
Philippines as shown in the Figure 2 below. Bansalan is known for its rich cultural diversity
and serves as one of the communities where the Bagobo Tagabawa people reside. The
municipality possesses significant cultural resources, traditions, and linguistic heritage
that contribute to the identity of the indigenous community.
Figure 2: Map of Bansalan
         Bansalan is about 73 kilometers (45 mi) south of Davao City and is very accessible

by land transportation mostly by buses going to Cotabato City, Kidapawan and Tacurong.
Bansalan is about 17 kilometers (11 mi) north-west of the provincial capital city of Digos.
The neighboring towns include Makilala in the north, Magsaysay in the west, Matanao in
the south and Mount Apo and part of Digos in the east.

         As modernization and globalization continue to influence local communities, there
is an increasing need to preserve and promote the Bagobo Tagabawa dialect and cultural
practices for future generations. The selection of Bansalan and some specific chosen
Barangay's as the locale of the study is appropriate because of the presence of the
Bagobo Tagabawa community and the availability of cultural knowledge that can support
the development and implementation of the proposed Web-Based Bagobo Tagabawa
Cultural Preservation System.

                                               System Design
         System design serves as the blueprint of the study, identifying the components,
interfaces, and data flow necessary for the development of the proposed system. Figure
3 illustrates the design framework that provides the foundation of the study by organizing
the components, interfaces, and data relationships needed in building the proposed
system.
Figure 3: Three Tier Architecture Model of the Study

         The three-tier architecture model was adopted in this study because it provides a
structured and efficient framework for developing the proposed system. It divides the
system into the presentation layer, business logic layer, and data layer, allowing each
component to perform a distinct function. The presentation layer manages the user
interface, the business logic layer handles the processing of system operations such as
content management, learning activities, and AI-assisted functions, while the data layer
is responsible for storing and retrieving the Bagobo Tagabawa cultural resources securely.

         This architecture is suitable for the study because it promotes modularity,
maintainability, and security. By separating the system into different layers, updates and
enhancements can be implemented more easily without affecting the entire system. It
also supports future expansion, making it appropriate for the long-term development of
the proposed Bagobo Tagabawa cultural preservation platform.
Recquirements
         Requirements planning is the phase in the development process where the

essential needs, functions, and constraints of the proposed system are identified,
analyzed, and organized. It establishes the foundation for the entire project by
determining what the system is expected to do, who will use it, and how it will support
the objectives of the study. Through this stage, the researchers are able to define the
scope of the system, clarify user requirements, and ensure that the proposed design is
aligned with the actual needs of the target community.

         In this study, requirements planning is important because it helps determine the
features that must be included in the Bagobo Tagabawa cultural preservation system,
such as the digital repository, learning modules, role-based access, and AI-assisted
support. It also allows the researchers to identify the technical and operational limitations
that may affect the development and implementation of the system. By carefully planning
the requirements, the study can produce a system that is practical, relevant, and
responsive to the needs of its intended users.

         System Recquirements
         System requirements define the necessary hardware, software, functional
capabilities, and operational conditions needed for the successful development and
implementation of the proposed system. They serve as a guide in identifying the
resources, technologies, and features required to ensure that the system performs
effectively, efficiently, and securely. Establishing clear system requirements helps
developers understand the expected functionalities of the system and provides a basis
for evaluating whether the system meets its intended objectives.

                  Software to be use. The development of the Epanaw Bagobo system will
utilize various software tools and technologies to support the implementation of the web-
based cultural preservation platform. The system will be developed using HTML,
JavaScript, and Bootstrap for the front-end interface, while PHP with the Laravel
Framework will be used for server-side processing and application logic. MySQL will serve
as the database management system for storing user information, cultural resources,
learning materials, and system records.

                  To support the AI-assisted learning component, an AI API service such as
OpenAI or Google Gemini will be integrated into the system to provide chatbot
functionality and learning assistance. The application will be hosted using Apache Web
Server through XAMPP during development and may be deployed. Development and
maintenance activities will be conducted using Visual Studio Code. Additional software
such as Audacity, Canva, and Microsoft Office applications will be utilized for multimedia
content preparation and project documentation.

Data Requirements
         Data requirements refer to the necessary information that the system must collect,
store, and manage in order to function properly. In this study, they include user data,
cultural content, learning materials, AI interaction data, and administrative records that
support the preservation and access of Bagobo Tagabawa dialect.

                  First Phase. For formality and acknowledgment, the researchers will ask
for certification in the NCIP office regarding data collection and system making ahead of
time.

                  Second Phase. The integration of the Epanaw Bagobo: An AI-Integrated
Website Framework for the Preservation and Revitalization of the Bagobo Tagabawa
Dialect and Cultural Heritage will be further explained after the confirmation of the NCIP
office for the gathering of data.

                  Third Phase. The researchers identify the specific data required by the
system, including cultural materials, language resources, user roles, learning content, and
administrative information. This phase helped determine the scope of the data to be
collected and ensured that the gathered information would support the objectives of the
study.

                  Fourth Phase. The researchers gathered data through interviews,
observations, and document review involving elders, community members, and other
relevant respondents. The collected information included Bagobo Tagabawa words,
stories, traditions, audio recordings, images, and other cultural references that would be
used in the digital repository and learning modules of the system.
                  Fifth Phase. After the data were collected, they were reviewed and
validated with the help of knowledgeable community members to ensure authenticity,
accuracy, and cultural relevance. The researchers then organized the data according to
category and purpose so that they could be properly stored and managed within the
system.

                  Sixth Phase. The validated data were then used as inputs for the
development of the proposed web-based system, particularly for the repository, learning
module, and AI-assisted features. This phase ensured that the system would reflect the
actual needs of the target users and would support the preservation and revitalization of
Bagobo Tagabawa culture and language.

illustrates the logical structure of the database for the proposed capstone. It identifies
the system's primary entities, their attributes, and the relationships among them. The
ERD serves as the blueprint for the database design by defining how data are organized,
stored, and interconnected to support the system's functionalities. It also ensures data
consistency, minimizes redundancy, and facilitates efficient data retrieval and
management. As shown in the ERD, the User Accounts entity serves as the central table
of the database since it manages the information of all registered users, including Super
Administrators, Administrators, and Users. Each user can access the system according to
the permissions assigned through the Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) mechanism. The
User Accounts entity is related to several modules, including Learning Progress, Quiz
Results, AI Chatbot Logs, Feedback, Community Contributions, and Activity Logs, allowing
the system to monitor user activities, learning records, chatbot interactions, submitted
contributions, and system usage. The Learning Materials entity stores the educational
content provided by the system, including language lessons and cultural learning
modules. Each learning material can be accessed by multiple users, while each user can
complete several lessons. The Learning Progress and Quiz Results entities are associated
with both User Accounts and Learning Materials to monitor lesson completion,
assessment scores, and overall learning performance. The Vocabulary Dictionary entity
contains the Bagobo Tagabawa vocabulary entries together with their English translations
and learning information. Each vocabulary entry is associated with the Pronunciation
Library, which stores verified audio recordings of native speakers. This relationship allows
users to learn not only the meaning of indigenous words but also their correct
pronunciation, thereby supporting the revitalization of the Bagobo Tagabawa dialect. The
Cultural Repository entity serves as the primary storage for digital cultural resources,
including documents, images, audio recordings, and videos related to the Bagobo
Tagabawa community. This repository is connected to the Multimedia Gallery, which
organizes multimedia files for easier browsing and presentation. Likewise, the repository
is linked to the Storytelling Archive, Cultural Practices, and Cultural Events entities,
enabling the system to preserve traditional stories, customs, rituals, ceremonies, festivals,
and other significant cultural information in a structured manner. To maintain the
authenticity and integrity of cultural resources, the Community Contributions entity
records the cultural materials submitted by registered users, while the Resource
Verification entity stores the validation records performed by administrators or recognized
cultural custodians. This process ensures that all published cultural resources have
undergone proper review before becoming accessible to other users. The AI Chatbot Logs
entity records user interactions with the AI-powered chatbot by storing users' questions
and the corresponding responses generated by the system. These records support system
monitoring and provide insights that can be used to improve the chatbot's performance.
Similarly, the Feedback entity collects users' ratings, comments, and suggestions
regarding the usability and effectiveness of the system. These evaluations provide
valuable information for the continuous enhancement of the platform. Finally, the Activity
Logs entity records significant actions performed by users and administrators, such as
uploading cultural resources, approving contributions, updating learning materials, and
managing user accounts. This entity supports administrative monitoring, accountability,
and system auditing by maintaining a complete record of important system activities.

entities required to support the proposed system. It provides a well-structured database
design that enables efficient management of user information, digital cultural resources,

participation, and administrative functions. Through these interconnected entities, the
proposed system effectively supports its primary objective of preserving and revitalizing
the Bagobo Tagabawa dialect and cultural heritage using a secure, organized, and AI-
integrated web-based platform.
